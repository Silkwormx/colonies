Aim the fan
Kepler learned A fan moves air. It does not make oxygen. Once we have air, we will still need this.

What a fan can do

A fan moves the air you already have. It does not make oxygen, and it does not take carbon dioxide out of a room. That is a different job, for a different machine.

Why move the air at all

In a closed habitat, the air a sleeper breathes out can collect close by if nothing is stirring it. Crew quarters on the International Space Station use fans to keep air moving while people sleep, so carbon dioxide does not build up near the face. A Mars habitat, which has gravity, can have still corners that go stale in the same way. Moving the air keeps it mixed. Kepler kept the fan for that job, once there is oxygen to move.
