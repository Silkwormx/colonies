Kepler learned Water splits into two parts hydrogen and one part oxygen. The oxygen is what you breathe. The hydrogen keeps for later.

The split

2 H2O → 2 H2 + O2

Electricity pries water apart. Two water molecules become two molecules of hydrogen and one of oxygen. That is why the wide tank holds twice what the narrow one does, and why a finished set is always two to one.

A day’s breath

A person uses about 0.84 kilograms of oxygen a day. Making that by electrolysis takes about 0.95 kilograms of water — a little under a liter. The rest of the mass is hydrogen, about 0.11 kilograms, which can be kept as fuel.

Why do this on Mars

Mars air is almost all carbon dioxide. There is no useful free oxygen in it. There is water ice. Melt it, run a current through it, and you get air. The same kind of splitter already makes oxygen on the International Space Station. It is not free: making one person’s daily oxygen takes several kilowatt-hours of electricity, so a habitat has to give the splitter a real share of its solar power. The hydrogen can be stored, or later combined with Martian carbon dioxide to make methane fuel.
