# Colonies — Mission 1: Can't Breathe

Kepler and Comet are sent ahead to make Mars livable before the rest of the colony arrives. The pod landed. The manual didn't. Six hours of oxygen.

**Characters:** Kepler (rust-red stitched scarf, calm) and Comet (brass goggles, eager).

---

## Scene 01 — Move-in Day
**Image:** `scene01.jpg`

Comet: "Five-star landing!"
Kepler: "Six hours of air."

**Science:** Mars air is about 95% carbon dioxide and far too thin to breathe.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — MARS AIR
Technically present. Not recommended. Surface pressure is about one percent of Earth's, which is a polite way of saying the sky is mostly a suggestion.

---

## Scene 02 — Fresh Air
**Image:** `scene02.jpg`

Comet: "Let's air the place out."
Kepler: "That's not airing out. That's checking out."

**Science:** One breath of Martian air would not sustain you. Keep the helmet on.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — HELMETS
On Mars, "fresh air" is a marketing term with no product behind it. The atmosphere is thin enough that even a polite breeze arrives late and underwhelmed.

---

## Scene 03 — First Principles
**Image:** `scene03.jpg`

Kepler scratches it in the dust. Need: oxygen. Have: ice, sunlight, scrap.
Comet: "Snacks."
Kepler: "Later."

**Science:** Oxygen can be made from water ice by electrolysis — or from CO₂, as NASA's MOXIE did on Perseverance.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — FIRST PRINCIPLES
Ask what the problem actually needs. Mars soil can contain perchlorates — salty compounds that are hostile to Earth microbes and inconvenient for farming. The planet restocks itself. The labels are missing.

---

## Scene 04 — Treasure
**Image:** `scene04.jpg`

Comet: "We're rich!"
Kepler: "We're breathing. Out here, same thing."

**Science:** Water ice lies under Martian dust in many places. The red color is iron oxide — basically rust.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — ICE
The most valuable substance on Mars. Also frozen water. Both are true. Dig under the rust and you may find the colony's first real paycheck.

---

## Scene 05 — The Fan
**Image:** `scene05.jpg`

Comet: "If the dust goes fast enough it's wind, and wind is basically air."
Kepler: "Three wrong things in one sentence."

**Science:** Blowing regolith around does not make oxygen. Chemistry does.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — THE FAN
The colony's first invention. It is no longer considered an invention. Mars dust is fine enough to get into everything; it does not, however, become breathable when accelerated.

---

## Scene 06 — More With Less
**Image:** `scene06.jpg`

Kepler: "Everything we need is already here. It's just in the wrong order."

**Science:** Electricity splits water into hydrogen and oxygen. A scavenged solar panel can run the current.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — ELECTROLYSIS
Sunlight in. Water split. Oxygen out. Perseverance's MOXIE later proved you can also wring oxygen from the CO₂ air itself — which is cheating, and therefore science.

---

## Scene 07 — The Bell
**Image:** `scene07.jpg`

Kepler hangs the one thing he packed from Earth: a small brass bell.
First ring: dull. Leak. They patch it.
Second ring: clear.
Kepler: "Seal holds."

**Science:** Ring test: a sealed structure rings clear; a crack sounds dull. Engineers on Earth do this too. In Mars' thin CO₂ air, sound is quieter, and high and low pitches travel at different speeds — so a bell can sound odd.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — RING TEST
A method of asking a building if it is okay. Buildings are surprisingly honest. On Mars, sound travels slower and arrives softer; even a good ring can sound like it took the scenic route.

---

## Scene 08 — First Breath
**Image:** `scene08.jpg`

Inside the sealed bubble, Comet takes his helmet off and breathes. A sprout beside him.
Comet: "Wrong planet?"
Kepler: "Right start."

**Science:** Habitats must stay sealed. Nothing grows in open Martian regolith — only inside a pressurized, protected space.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — FIRST BREATH
Available indoors only. Outdoors remains a museum exhibit. The sprout agrees, which is why it lives in glass.

---

## Scene 09 — Someday
**Image:** `scene09.jpg`

Blue sunset. Kepler sketches a giant tree under a dome in the dust.
Kepler: "Someday, a forest under glass."
Horizon: weather coming.
**Next: Too Cold.**

**Science:** Fine dust makes Mars sunsets blue near the sun. The colony still needs heat, shielding, and power before any forest under glass.

### THE COLONY GUIDE — BLUE SUNSETS
On Mars, dusk can turn blue near the sun while the rest of the sky stays peach. Fine dust scatters the light. The planet has a sense of irony, and a dust storm waiting on the horizon.

## Series note
Each mission builds toward the lush banyan-dome colony. Mission 1 unlocks air. The brass bell stays.
