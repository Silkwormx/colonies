Can't Breathe

Kepler and Comet are sent ahead to make Mars livable. The pod landed. The manual didn't. Six hours of oxygen.

Scroll ↓

Comet: “Five-star landing!”

Kepler: “Six hours of air.”

Mars air is about 95% carbon dioxide and far too thin to breathe.

The Colony Guide
Mars Air

Technically present. Not recommended.

Surface pressure is less than one percent of Earth's, which is a polite way of saying the sky is mostly a suggestion.

Comet: “Let's air the place out.”

Kepler: “That's not airing out. That's checking out.”

One breath of Martian air would not sustain you. Keep the helmet on.

The Colony Guide
Helmets

On Mars, “fresh air” is a marketing term with no product behind it.

The atmosphere is thin enough that even a polite breeze arrives late and underwhelmed.

Need: oxygen. Have: ice, sunlight, scrap.

Comet: “Snacks.”

Kepler: “Later.”

Oxygen can be made from water ice by electrolysis — or from CO₂, as NASA's MOXIE did on Perseverance.

First Principles

Ask what the problem actually needs.

Mars soil can contain perchlorates — salty compounds hostile to Earth microbes and inconvenient for farming.

The planet restocks itself. The labels are missing.

Comet: “We're rich!”

Kepler: “We're breathing. Out here, same thing.”

Water ice lies under Martian dust in many places. The red color is iron oxide — basically rust.

The Colony Guide
Ice

The most valuable substance on Mars. Also frozen water. Both are true.

Dig under the rust and you may find the colony's first real paycheck.

Comet: “If the dust goes fast enough it's wind, and wind is basically air.”

Kepler: “Three wrong things in one sentence.”

Blowing regolith around does not make oxygen. Chemistry does.

The Colony Guide
The Fan

The colony's first invention. It is no longer considered an invention.

Mars dust is fine enough to get into everything; it does not, however, become breathable when accelerated.

Kepler: “Everything we need is already here. It's just in the wrong order.”

Electricity splits water into hydrogen and oxygen. A scavenged solar panel can run the current.

The Colony Guide
Electrolysis

Sunlight in. Water split. Oxygen out.

Perseverance's MOXIE later proved you can also wring oxygen from the CO₂ air itself — which is cheating, and therefore science.

First ring: dull. Leak. They patch it.

Second ring: clear.

Kepler: “Seal holds.”

Ring test: a sealed structure rings clear; a crack sounds dull. On Mars, sound is quieter and high/low pitches travel at different speeds.

The Colony Guide
Ring Test

A method of asking a building if it is okay. Buildings are surprisingly honest.

On Mars, sound travels slower and arrives softer; even a good ring can sound like it took the scenic route.

Kepler: “Right start.”

Habitats must stay sealed. Nothing grows in open Martian regolith — only inside a pressurized, protected space.

First Breath

Available indoors only. Outdoors remains a museum exhibit.

The sprout agrees, which is why it lives in glass.

Kepler: “Someday, a forest under glass.”

Horizon: weather coming.

Fine dust makes Mars sunsets blue near the sun. Heat, shielding, and power still needed before any forest under glass.

The Colony Guide
Blue Sunsets

On Mars, dusk can turn blue near the sun while the rest of the sky stays peach. Fine dust scatters the light.

The planet has a sense of irony, and a dust storm waiting on the horizon.
